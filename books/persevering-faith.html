Persevering Faith

There is a kind of faith that begins well, and there is a kind that finishes well and strong. The difference between the two is perseverance.
“Being confident of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ.”
Philippians 1 : 6 · NKJV

Introduction

Faith perseveres when it refuses to stop, regardless of the impossibilities confronting it. There is a kind of faith that begins well, and there is a kind of faith that finishes well and strong. The difference between the two is perseverance.

Many start with passion, but only a few continue when the road becomes difficult. Many respond to a call, but fewer remain when the weight of that call presses heavily on their shoulders. Persevering Faith is about that rare strength that refuses to quit, that stays when others withdraw, that builds when others abandon the work.

The story of Nehemiah stands as an instructive witness to this kind of faith. He did not begin with tools in his hands. He began with a burden in his heart. When he heard that the walls of Jerusalem were broken and its gates burned with fire, he did not turn away. He sat down, wept, fasted, and prayed before the God of heaven (Nehemiah 1:3–4). Great works for God are not first built with hands. They are first built in the heart through faith.

From that place of prayer, Nehemiah rose to act. When he arrived in Jerusalem, he assessed the damage quietly, counted the cost, and then spoke words that stirred a discouraged people. "Let us rise up and build" (Nehemiah 2:18). Yet the moment the work began, opposition arose. This is the nature of every divine assignment. The presence of opposition is not a sign that you are out of God's will. It is often a sign that you are exactly where you are meant to be.

As the work progressed, the resistance intensified. External threats were joined by internal struggles. This is where persevering faith proves its worth. It does not deny the presence of weakness. It rises above it. Nehemiah did not abandon the work. He reorganized the people. He placed guards. He reminded them of God's support. They worked with one hand and held a weapon with the other. Progress continued, not because conditions improved, but because faith remained.

In the end, the wall was completed. "So the wall was finished in fifty-two days" (Nehemiah 6:15). What opposition could not stop, what discouragement could not silence, and what weakness could not destroy, God brought to completion. This book is built on that testimony. It reminds us that what God begins, He intends to complete. The walls you are called to build may not be made of stone. They may be your family, your calling, your ministry, your purpose. If you will stay with God, stand through opposition, and refuse to come down, you will see completion because in the hands of God, persevering faith always finishes.

Chapter 1

Burden Before Breakthrough

MFM did not begin as a global ministry. It began quietly, almost unnoticed, as an informal prayer gathering of about fifteen people in 1989 in the living room of Dr. Daniel Kolawole Olukoya, the General Overseer. Yet what it lacked in size, it carried in fire. From that small beginning, growth came swiftly and steadily until the group expanded into a congregation of hundreds of thousands, spreading across nations and continents.

This pattern reflects a consistent scriptural principle. "Do not despise the day of small things" (Zechariah 4:10). What God begins in obscurity, He often establishes in glory. The early days may appear insignificant, but they carry the seed of something far greater.

In those formative years, precisely in 1994, MFM moved to its permanent site at 13 Olasimbo Street. To the natural eye, it was an unlikely place for anything meaningful to emerge. But God specializes in building greatness out of what others overlook. This transformation reflects the truth of Scripture: "The stone which the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone" (Psalm 118:22).

When A Ruin Becomes A Prophecy

Every divine project begins as a burden before it becomes a breakthrough. Before walls rise, something must first break within. That was the pattern in Jerusalem in Nehemiah's day. "When I heard these words, that I sat down and wept, and mourned for many days; I was fasting and praying before the God of heaven" (Nehemiah 1:4). Burden is the birthplace of persevering faith.

MFM Kubwa did not begin as a cathedral. It began quietly as a small fellowship. Yet, there was fire. In May 2016, a new chapter opened with my deployment to the region as Senior Regional Overseer. What I met was not a finished work, but a structure that had served its time. Like Nehemiah standing before broken walls, I discerned that the next level required a bold step.

Then came the moment that defined the journey. The old structure was demolished. To the natural eye, it looked like a loss. To the eye of faith, it was preparation. Standing over that rubble, with only a handful of prayer ministers and a bottle of anointing oil, I prophesied. I declared that within nine months, a new, ultra-modern structure would rise in place of the ruins. Faith speaks before it sees.

When the People Have a Mind to Work

That declaration did not come in a season of abundance. It came in a season of limitation. The church treasury held less than one million naira. The gap between the prophecy and the reality was wide, and many doubted its possibility. Just as in Nehemiah's time, when voices rose against the work, saying, "What are these feeble Jews doing?" (Nehemiah 4:2), skepticism surrounded the vision.

But persevering faith does not consult doubt for direction. It returns to God. Prayer became the foundation. Unity became our strength. Sacrifice became the language of the people. Members gave, not out of abundance, but out of conviction. "And the people had a mind to work" (Nehemiah 4:6). Day by day, progress was made. The prophetic word began to take visible shape.

Then came the testimony that silenced doubt. The project was completed in eight months, not nine. God not only honored the word, but He also exceeded our expectation. "So the wall was finished, and it happened, when all our enemies heard of it, that they perceived that this work was done by our God" (Nehemiah 6:15–16). What began as rubble became a revelation.

The Essence of Persevering Faith

Today, MFM Kubwa stands as more than a structure. It is a testimony of faith that refused to bow to limitation, of prayer that refused to grow silent, of obedience that refused to delay. It stands as visible proof that when God plants a burden in a heart, and that burden is carried with persevering faith, no opposition, no lack, and no timeline can stop its fulfillment.

This is the essence of persevering faith: It sees beyond ruins. It speaks in the face of lack. It stands when others doubt. It builds when others retreat. And in the end, it finishes.

As the Bible says, "Being confident of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it" (Philippians 1:6). Sometimes your placement may not reflect your purpose. Yet faithfulness in that place remains essential. "Moreover it is required in stewards that one be found faithful" (1 Corinthians 4:2). In God's timing, both the work and the workers are revealed.

Chapter 2

Prayer That Opens Doors

The quiet place of prayer prepares the way for the public steps of faith. What appears later as courage in the open is often the result of long hours spent alone with God. Before David stood before Goliath, he had stood in lonely fields with a sling and a heart trained to trust God. Before Nehemiah spoke before the king, he had already wept, fasted, and prayed in secret. Public victories are rarely sudden.

Faith may begin with a word from God, but it is prayer that sustains it, strengthens it, and carries it to maturity. "So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God" (Romans 10:17). Yet the same faith must be nurtured through communion with God. It is in prayer that doubts are silenced, direction becomes clear, and courage is renewed.

Jesus Himself lived by this pattern. "But Jesus Himself often withdrew into the wilderness and prayed" (Luke 5:16). If the Son of God did not rely on impulse but on prayer, then no one can sustain a life of faith without it. Prayer is not an accessory to faith. It is its lifeline.

Prayer That Aligns Time And Steps

Before Nehemiah stood before a king, he had already stood before God. The request he would later make in a palace was first formed in a place of tears, fasting, and steady prayer. Nehemiah 2:4–5 reveals how prayer precedes the steps of faith, saying, "So I prayed to the God of heaven. And I said to the king." Even in a tense moment before the king, he could pause within and reach God without delay.

Nehemiah did not speak too early. He did not move too late. Four months passed between his first prayer and his conversation with the king. That waiting was not empty. It was alignment. Prayer has a way of adjusting both the person and the timing. "Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things, which you do not know" (Jeremiah 33:3).

Many miss doors because they move without asking. Others miss them because they wait without seeking. Nehemiah prayed and then moved. That balance is where persevering faith grows.

Strength For The People Who Build

Nehemiah did not pray alone. He carried the people with him. When fear began to spread among the workers, he did not ignore it. He addressed it and pointed them back to God. "Do not be afraid of them. Remember the Lord, great and awesome, and fight for your brethren" (Nehemiah 4:14).

Prayer strengthens more than plans. It strengthens people. It steadies tired hands. It lifts heavy hearts. I remember speaking with a member who gave beyond what seemed reasonable for his situation. He said quietly, "Sir, I prayed about it. I have peace." That is the work of God in a person's heart. Prayer had done what words alone could not do.

Prayer does not remove pressure. It gives you a steady heart within it. "Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God" (Philippians 4:6). Not some things. Everything. That includes budgets, timelines, and decisions that look purely practical.

From Request To Result

Nehemiah asked for letters, for timber, and for safe passage. What he received matched his request. That was not luck. It was the result of prayer shaping his ask and God opening the way. "And the king granted them to me according to the good hand of my God upon me" (Nehemiah 2:8).

Persevering faith is not built on a single prayer. It is sustained by a life of prayer. It is the steady returning to God for guidance, strength, and clarity. There is a place for planning. There is a place for strategy. Yet without prayer, both can drift. With prayer, both are anchored.

Nehemiah prayed before he spoke. He prayed while he worked. He prayed when he faced threats. Prayer was not an event in his journey. It was the thread that held everything together. Before major steps, I return to God. During the work, I return yet again. After completion, I return with thanks. That pattern keeps the heart right and the path clear.

Chapter 3

Rising To Build Amidst Resistance

Every call of God is answered in an imperfect moment. The setting is often uneasy, the risks are real, and the timing does not wait for our comfort. Yet Scripture shows a steady pattern. What God appoints becomes right because He appoints it. The moment is not made perfect by favorable conditions. It is made right by God's choice.

Moses did not receive his assignment in a place of ease. He had fled Egypt as a fugitive. Returning meant facing the past, the power of Pharaoh, and the fear of rejection by his own people. Yet God did not change the moment. He strengthened the man for the moment. "I will certainly be with you" (Exodus 3:12). The Red Sea did not part before he obeyed. It parted after he stepped forward.

Esther's moment came in a place of influence, yet it carried the weight of danger. Then Mordecai spoke words that revealed the mystery of God's timing. "Yet who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (Esther 4:14). Esther chose to act. "I will go to the king, and if I perish, I perish!" (Esther 4:16). Her survival was not because the risk disappeared. It was because God had chosen that moment.

Recognizing The Moment God Chooses

Many wait for a moment that feels right. They look for stability, clarity, and comfort. Yet the moments God chooses often carry tension, uncertainty, and pressure. If Moses had waited for Egypt to become welcoming, he would have waited in vain. If Esther had waited for safety, her people would have been lost.

The right moment is not defined by human conditions. It is defined by divine selection. "To everything there is a season, a time for every purpose under heaven" (Ecclesiastes 3:1). Nehemiah did not wait for calm weather. He rose while the walls were still broken and the city still exposed. "Come and let us build the wall of Jerusalem, that we may no longer be a reproach" (Nehemiah 2:17).

Then came the response that set the work in motion. "Let us rise up and build. Then they set their hands to this good work" (Nehemiah 2:18). Faith moved before conditions improved. That is the pattern of persevering faith. It does not wait for ease. It responds to purpose.

Voices That Resist The Work

Opposition did not take long to appear. "But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite official, and Geshem the Arab heard of it, they laughed at us and despised us" (Nehemiah 2:19). Every assignment that carries God's mark attracts resistance. "For a great and effective door has opened to me, and there are many adversaries" (1 Corinthians 16:9). The open door and the presence of opposition often appear together.

Resistance is not always a signal to stop. It can be a sign that the work matters. Nehemiah did not argue with every critic. He returned to the work. "The God of heaven Himself will prosper us. Therefore we His servants will arise and build" (Nehemiah 2:20). Courage is not loud. It is steady. It shows up again the next day and the day after that.

I remember a season in my own work when discouraging words came from unexpected places. It would have been easy to pause and reconsider everything. Instead, I returned to prayer and then to the work. Progress was not dramatic at first, but it was consistent. Faith does not wait for encouragement before it moves. It moves and finds encouragement along the way.

Strength That Grows In Unity

The work did not belong to Nehemiah alone. It belonged to the people. Families took sections of the wall. Progress came through shared effort. "So we built the wall, for the people had a mind to work" (Nehemiah 4:6). Unity is not the absence of difference. It is an agreement on purpose.

A simple picture makes this clear. One person trying to lift a heavy beam will struggle. Two or three working together can raise it with less strain. The load has not changed. The strength applied to it has increased. In seasons of resistance, unity becomes even more important. It keeps the work moving when individuals feel tired.

The wall did not rise in a single day. It rose through repeated effort. Stone after stone. Section after section. Each day added something. Resistance remained, but it did not halt the work. Persevering faith is not measured by a single act of courage. It is seen in continued movement over time.

Chapter 4

Holding Tools And Weapons Together

Undertaking any project for God through persevering faith does not ignore danger. Rather, persevering faith recognizes the danger and confronts it headlong. The wall in Jerusalem did not rise in a quiet season. It rose under pressure. Progress was real. Then the threats grew louder. Plots formed. Rumors spread. Fear crept into the camp.

Nehemiah did not stop the work. He changed how the work was done. "Those who built on the wall, with one hand they worked at construction, and with the other held a weapon" (Nehemiah 4:17). Hands were busy with labor. Eyes were open to danger. Hearts were fixed on God. Persevering faith does not pretend that danger is absent. It refuses to let danger halt the assignment.

Progress With Vigilance

It is possible to be so focused on building that you forget to watch. It is also possible to be so focused on threats that you forget to build. Nehemiah refused both extremes. He kept the people working while placing guards, setting watches, and keeping them alert. "Every one of the builders had his sword girded at his side as he built" (Nehemiah 4:18). That is balance. Not panic. Not carelessness.

The resistance Nehemiah faced was not only physical. It carried spiritual weight. "For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against spiritual hosts of wickedness" (Ephesians 6:12). Nehemiah addressed both dimensions. He set physical guards. He also called the people to remember God. Spiritual awareness strengthens physical effort. When the heart is steady, the hands can continue their work.

Not all resistance came from outside. Some of it rose from within. "The strength of the laborers is failing, and there is so much rubbish that we are not able to build the wall" (Nehemiah 4:10). These were not enemies speaking. These were workers. Every long project reaches this point. Energy dips. Nehemiah did not dismiss their concerns. He shifted their focus from the size of the problem to the greatness of God.

Clothed For The Work

The New Testament gives language to what Nehemiah practiced in principle. "Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil" (Ephesians 6:11). Armor does not replace the work. It protects the worker. Truth guards the mind. Righteous living guards the heart. Faith shields against fear. God's Word keeps direction clear.

Peter gives direct instructions: "Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour" (1 Peter 5:8). During seasons of building, it is easy to relax too soon. Progress can create a false sense of safety. Nehemiah did not allow that. Even at night, he kept watch.

In the midst of pressure, God's word becomes an anchor. "Fear not, for I am with you. Be not dismayed, for I am your God" (Isaiah 41:10). This promise does not remove opposition. It addresses the heart within it. A child walking through a dark place may feel fear. The presence of a parent changes the experience. God's presence carries that same effect.

Discipline That Sustains Momentum

Momentum is fragile. It can be lost through neglect, distraction, or disorder. Nehemiah maintained order through discipline. He assigned roles. He placed families together. He ensured that everyone knew their part. Structure helped the work continue. Paul echoes this principle: "I discipline my body and bring it into subjection" (1 Corinthians 9:27).

I recall a period during a building project when progress was steady, yet tension was rising. Costs were increasing. There were also subtle distractions pulling attention away from the core work. At one point, we paused to pray. That moment brought focus back. Progress without spiritual awareness can lead to costly mistakes. Awareness without progress leads to stagnation. Both must walk together.

The wall was built because the people refused to choose between working and watching. They did both. Persevering faith holds tools and weapons together: It builds with one hand. It guards with the other hand. It moves forward without losing awareness. That balance keeps the work alive until it is finished.

Chapter 5

Finishing Strong And Guarding The Victory

Persevering faith will start and complete any project God puts in your hands. Many begin with energy. Fewer finish with strength. Nehemiah's account stands out because the work did not stop halfway. It reached completion. "So the wall was finished in fifty two days" (Nehemiah 6:15).

That statement carries more than a timeline. It reveals endurance. It reveals focus sustained under pressure. Completion matters to God. It reflects His nature. He begins, and He completes. "Being confident of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it" (Philippians 1:6). Persevering faith does not celebrate the start alone. It presses forward until the assignment is done.

Refusing Distraction

The closer Nehemiah came to finishing, the more intense the distractions became. Invitations were sent. Messages were crafted to pull him away from the wall. "Come, let us meet together" (Nehemiah 6:2). At first glance, the request sounded reasonable. Yet Nehemiah saw beyond the surface. "But they thought to do me harm" (Nehemiah 6:2).

His reply has become a standard for focus. "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down" (Nehemiah 6:3). He did not argue. He stated his priority and stayed with it. Distraction often appears near the finish line. It comes dressed as urgency. Yet its goal is to break concentration and delay completion.

A runner nearing the end of a race does not stop to greet spectators. The finish line demands attention. The opposition did not stop at invitations. False reports followed. "They were all trying to make us afraid" (Nehemiah 6:9). Nehemiah responded in a brief prayer. "Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands" (Nehemiah 6:9). Strength is not always a feeling. It is often a response to reliance on God.

Guarding What Has Been Built

Finishing the wall was not the end of Nehemiah's responsibility. It was the beginning of another phase. "Now it came to pass, when the wall was built, that I appointed the gatekeepers, the singers, and the Levites" (Nehemiah 7:1). Victory must be guarded. A house completed without doors remains exposed. What is built must be preserved.

Nehemiah understood that success without structure can quickly decline. He placed responsible people over the city. "I gave the charge of Jerusalem to my brother Hanani, for he was a faithful man and feared God more than many" (Nehemiah 7:2). Faithfulness became a key qualification. Not position. Not status. Character.

Sustaining a work requires people who can be trusted. It requires order that keeps things functioning. A business may grow quickly, but without proper systems, it can collapse under its own weight. Structure holds growth in place.

A Life That Finishes Well

Paul echoed the same principle at the end of his journey. "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith" (2 Timothy 4:7). The fight speaks of resistance. The race speaks of endurance. Keeping the faith speaks of preservation. "Let us run with endurance the race that is set before us" (Hebrews 12:1).

Another life that reflects this pattern is Job. His fight came through loss that touched every part of his life. Yet Job responded with restraint and conviction. "Though He slay me, yet will I trust Him" (Job 13:15). His restoration did not come at the beginning. It came after he had endured. His latter end was greater than his beginning.

Joseph's journey also reflects this pattern. His fight began with betrayal. He fought by maintaining his character. He endured through years of waiting. "The word of the LORD tested him" (Psalm 105:19). When the moment of fulfillment arrived, he was ready. "So it was not you who sent me here, but God" (Genesis 45:8). Paul, Job, and Joseph each faced different forms of resistance. Yet the pattern remains the same. They fought. They endured. They kept their faith. Finishing is not the end of work. It is the beginning of stewardship. Persevering faith does more than start. It stays, it focuses, it completes, and it guards.

Prayers & Declarations

Prayer Points

  1. 1O God, arise and plant in me the burden that gives birth to divine breakthroughs; let every spirit of complacency and spiritual lukewarmness die by fire, in Jesus' name.
  2. 2Powers assigned to make me abandon my God-given assignment halfway, your time is up, die by fire, in Jesus' name.
  3. 3Every voice of discouragement, mockery, and unbelief speaking against my destiny and divine projects, be silenced forever, in Jesus' name.
  4. 4My Father, my Father, baptize me with the spirit of persevering faith that refuses to quit in the face of adversity, in Jesus' name.
  5. 5Every enemy of my prayer life, every power assigned to weaken my altar of prayer, be consumed by the fire of the Holy Ghost, in Jesus' name.
  6. 6O Lord, align my steps with Your timing and order my ways according to Your perfect will; let me not move ahead of You or lag behind Your purpose, in Jesus' name.
  7. 7Every Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem assigned against the work of my hands, scatter unto desolation and perish by fire, in Jesus' name.
  8. 8Powers sponsoring fear, confusion, and internal discouragement against my destiny, lose your hold over my life and die, in Jesus' name.
  9. 9O Lord, grant me the grace to rise and build in difficult seasons, and let no opposition stop the fulfillment of Your purpose for my life, in Jesus' name.
  10. 10Every arrow of distraction, compromise, and diversion fired against my calling and destiny, backfire by fire, in Jesus' name.
  11. 11My Father, teach my hands to build and my spirit to watch; clothe me with divine wisdom, discernment, and spiritual alertness, in Jesus' name.
  12. 12Every power waiting for me to relax so as to steal, kill, or destroy what You have given me, be disappointed and perish, in Jesus' name.
  13. 13O God of Nehemiah, strengthen my hands for the work, renew my strength daily, and let every good work You have committed into my hands prosper, in Jesus' name.
  14. 14Every spirit of unfinished projects, failure at the edge of breakthrough, and abandonment of divine assignments, come out of my life and die, in Jesus' name.
  15. 15O Lord, by Your mercy, grant me the grace not only to start well but to finish strong, preserve every victory You have given me, and let my latter end be greater than my beginning, in Jesus' name.
